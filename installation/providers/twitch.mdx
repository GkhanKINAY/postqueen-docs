On a self-hosted PostQueen the Twitch app is yours. You register it in the Twitch developer console and give PostQueen its client ID and secret. Twitch does not review apps.

## Before you start

| | |
|---|---|
| Developer portal | [Twitch developer console](https://dev.twitch.tv/console), with two-factor authentication on your Twitch account |
| Variables | `TWITCH_CLIENT_ID`, `TWITCH_CLIENT_SECRET` |
| Redirect URI | `FRONTEND_URL` + `/integrations/social/twitch` |
| Scopes | `user:write:chat`, `user:read:chat`, `moderator:manage:announcements` |
| Review | None |

Scopes are not set on the app form: PostQueen asks for the three when a channel connects, and does not check which were granted. `user:write:chat` sends chat messages and `moderator:manage:announcements` sends announcements, so approve all three on Twitch's screen.

## Create the app

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on two-factor authentication">
    Twitch lets you register apps only once two-factor authentication is on. Verify your email, turn it on under **Security and Privacy** in your Twitch settings, then open the [developer console](https://dev.twitch.tv/console).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register the application">
    Open **Applications** and choose **Register Your Application**:

    - **Name**: a name no other Twitch app uses, such as `PostQueen Example Inc`.
    - **OAuth Redirect URLs**: your address from the next step. Choose **Add** after typing it.
    - **Category**: pick the closest one.
    - **Client Type**: **Confidential**. A public client gets no secret, and the type cannot be changed later.

    Then choose **Create**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the redirect URL">
    Use the address that matches how you run PostQueen:

    <CodeGroup>

    ```text Your domain
    https://postqueen.example.com/integrations/social/twitch
    ```

    ```text Local development
    http://localhost:4200/integrations/social/twitch
    ```

    ```text Docker on your machine
    http://localhost:4007/integrations/social/twitch
    ```

    </CodeGroup>

    Twitch accepts `https` addresses, and plain `http` for `localhost`.

    <OAuthRedirect />
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the client ID and a new secret">
    In **Developer Applications**, choose **Manage** on the app. Copy the **Client ID**, then choose **New Secret** and copy the secret. A new secret replaces the previous one, so update your environment whenever you make one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Set the variables

Add the two keys to your environment:

```env
TWITCH_CLIENT_ID="your client id"
TWITCH_CLIENT_SECRET="your client secret"
```

PostQueen sends the client ID with every Twitch call, so a channel stops working if you clear it later.

<RestartAndAddChannel />

Pick **Twitch**, sign in, and approve the chat permissions. Posts go to the chat of the channel you signed in with.

## Chat limits

In your own chat you can send 100 messages every 30 seconds, and one announcement every 2 seconds. PostQueen sends one Twitch message at a time and waits 2 seconds before each.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Redirection URLs must use HTTPS protocol">
    Twitch refused the redirect URL on the app form. Use an `https` address, or `http://localhost` for a local install, and make sure no empty URL row is left on the form.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Twitch says the redirect_uri does not match">
    The address PostQueen sent is not in **OAuth Redirect URLs**. It is exactly your `FRONTEND_URL` followed by `/integrations/social/twitch`.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Chat messages go out, announcements do not">
    The token lacks `moderator:manage:announcements`. Twitch does not confirm an announcement, so PostQueen records it as posted anyway. Reconnect the channel and approve every permission.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
