Friday afternoon, one click, and the week is already counted. **Analytics** in the left menu opens on the first channel in the list with a card for every metric that platform will part with, a small chart in each, and one big number underneath.

Then the honest part. She posts to more than 30 networks. Ten of them send numbers back.

## Ten channels report back

Posting is the wide part of PostQueen. Measuring is the narrow one, and the line is drawn by the platforms, not by her. These ten answer when she asks:

| Channel | Windows you can pick | Per-post numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook Page | 7, 30 or 90 days | Yes |
| Google My Business | 7, 30 or 90 days | No |
| Instagram (Facebook Business) | 7 or 30 days | Yes |
| Instagram (Standalone) | 7 or 30 days | Yes |
| LinkedIn Page | 7, 30 or 90 days | Yes |
| Pinterest | 7, 30 or 90 days | Yes |
| Threads | 7 or 30 days | Yes |
| TikTok | 7 or 30 days | Yes |
| X (Twitter) | 7, 30 or 90 days | Yes |
| YouTube | 7, 30 or 90 days | Yes |

Every other connector posts perfectly well and reports nothing. Reddit, Discord and Mastodon publish on time and stay silent afterwards, so a blank analytics view there is the platform being quiet rather than your install being broken. Those channels do not appear in the Analytics sidebar at all.

Two more things catch people out. LinkedIn personal profiles are not on the list, only LinkedIn Pages report back. And Google My Business answers at the location level, which is why it has channel numbers but no per-post ones.

<Note>
Set `DISABLE_X_ANALYTICS` and she stops asking X for numbers entirely. The X channel drops out of the Analytics sidebar and the Statistics icon disappears from X posts on the calendar. Useful when your X plan does not include analytics access. See [X (Twitter)](/providers/x-twitter).
</Note>

## What each channel returns

The card labels come straight from what each platform is willing to say. Pinterest talks about pins, YouTube talks about watch time, and Google My Business talks about people asking for directions.

| Channel | Metrics on the channel page |
|---|---|
| Facebook Page | Page Impressions, Media views, Posts Engagement, Page followers |
| Google My Business | Website Clicks, Phone Calls, Direction Requests, Desktop Map Views, Mobile Map Views |
| Instagram (both connectors) | Follower Count, Reach, Likes, Views, Comments, Shares, Saves, Replies |
| LinkedIn Page | Page Views, Clicks, Shares, Engagement, Comments, Organic Followers, Paid Followers |
| Pinterest | Pin click rate, Impressions, Pin Clicks, Engagement, Saves |
| Threads | Views, Likes, Replies, Reposts, Quotes |
| TikTok | Followers, Following, Total Likes, Videos, Views, Recent Likes, Recent Comments, Recent Shares |
| X (Twitter) | IMPRESSION, BOOKMARK, LIKE, QUOTE, REPLY, RETWEET |
| YouTube | Estimated Minutes Watched, Average View Duration, Average View Percentage, Subscribers Gained, Subscribers Lost, Likes |

Two of those rows deserve a footnote.

TikTok splits into account figures and video figures. The first four describe the account as it stands today. The four that follow, starting at Views, are added up across your twenty most recent videos, so they move as you publish rather than tracking the window you picked.

X shouts. Those labels arrive in capitals because they are built from the raw metric names X returns, and she prints them as they come.

## Reading a card

Each card carries the metric name, a chart of the window, and one large total.

The total adds up every point in the window. Two YouTube metrics are averaged across the days instead of summed: Average View Duration and Average View Percentage. A chart is drawn for every metric that came back with at least one point, single figures included. Only a metric that came back with nothing at all loses its chart, and what you get then is a lone centered zero.

<Note>
Some channels also paint a small arrow beside the metric name. Treat the chart and the total as the numbers of record.
</Note>

## The refresh control

A channel that shows nothing but a clock and the line *This channel needs to be refreshed to display analytics* is not broken. Its connection to the platform has gone stale, and the **Refresh Channel** button underneath sends you back through that platform's own login to fix it. You land back in PostQueen with the numbers flowing again.

The same thing announces itself in the sidebar. A channel that needs attention wears a red exclamation mark, and clicking it warns you to refresh the integration from the calendar first.

Behind the scenes she tries to spare you that trip. When a stored token has expired she quietly renews it before asking for numbers, and only falls back to the visible refresh when renewal is impossible.

<Warning>
If the renewal fails outright, she disconnects the channel rather than leaving it half alive. Reconnect it from the calendar and the history comes back with it.
</Warning>

Channel numbers are cached for one hour per channel and per window. Flicking between 7 and 30 days is instant, and a figure you are reading may be up to an hour old. Self-hosting in development mode? The cache drops to one second, so you see every call land.

## Per-post statistics

Hover a post on the calendar and a small chart icon appears, labeled **Post Statistics**. Click it and a **Statistics** window opens on that one post.

It has its own window picker with 7, 30 and 90 days, and one caveat worth knowing before you reach for it: only LinkedIn Page turns that window into a day-by-day series. Every other network answers with the post's numbers as they stand right now, so the picker changes nothing on those cards. Post numbers are also read live on every open, never cached, so what you see is what the platform said a second ago.

| Channel | Metrics on a single post |
|---|---|
| Facebook Page | Impressions, Reactions, Clicks, Clicks by Type |
| Instagram (both connectors) | Views, Reach, Saves, Likes, Comments, Shares |
| LinkedIn Page | Impressions, Unique Impressions, Clicks, Likes, Comments, Shares, Engagement |
| Pinterest | Impressions, Pin Clicks, Outbound Clicks, Saves |
| Threads | Views, Likes, Replies, Reposts, Quotes |
| TikTok | Views, Likes, Comments, Shares |
| X (Twitter) | Impressions, Likes, Retweets, Replies, Quotes, Bookmarks |
| YouTube | Views, Likes, Comments, Favorites |

Pinterest answers for the last 90 days only, on the channel page and on the post, because that is as far back as Pinterest will look.

### When a post cannot find itself

Sometimes a platform accepts a post and then declines to say which post it became. The Statistics window notices, and instead of an empty chart it shows you a grid of your recent content from that platform with a simple instruction: *Select the content that matches this post.* Pick the right one, press **Connect Post**, and the statistics open immediately afterwards.

The Post Statistics icon only shows up on posts that have already published, so a scheduled post or a draft has no window to open in the first place.

## Short link clicks

You put a link in the post. Someone read the post. Did anyone press the link?

The Statistics window answers that underneath the metric cards, in a **Short Links Statistics** table with three columns:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Short Link | The shortened URL she generated for this post |
| Original Link | Where it points |
| Clicks | How many times it was opened |

This section fills in only when a link shortener is configured on your install. She picks the first one whose key is present, in this order:

| Shortener | Key |
|---|---|
| Dub | `DUB_TOKEN` |
| Short.io | `SHORT_IO_SECRET_KEY` |
| Kutt | `KUTT_API_KEY` |
| LinkDrip | `LINK_DRIP_API_KEY` |

With none of them set, the table reads *No short link results* and nothing is being tracked. Counted links are the ones on your shortener's own domain, found in the post text, including the follow-up posts in a thread. A raw link you typed by hand and left alone is not counted, because there is nothing in the middle to count it.
